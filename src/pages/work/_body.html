Six months as the working lead of a startup's commerce team

Since April 2026 I have been team lead for the commerce product at a US telehealth startup. I led a six-person offshore engineering team, wrote most of the specifications they built from, and shipped and supported a large share of the code myself. This page sets out what the record shows.

The job

The company is a startup, so the role covered three kinds of work at once.

Lead

I turned product requirement documents into epics and tickets the team could build from, ran estimation and sprint planning, and reviewed and merged the team's code.

Build

I shipped my own features and fixes across the platform API, the patient storefront and the clinic admin app.

Own production

I ran deploys, on-call and production data repairs, and found the root cause when something broke.

Selected work

Each item is marked Built where I wrote the code, Led where the team built it from my specification and review, and Investigated where I found the cause and directed the fix.

Building an in-house commerce platform

Context
The storefront ran on WooCommerce behind the scenes, and subscription renewals depended on its webhooks. The company wanted it gone without breaking live clinics.
What I did
I specified the epic: a BullMQ renewal engine with its own scheduler, worker, failure alerts and daily recovery sweep, plus a way to migrate each clinic. One engineer on my team built most of it. I wrote the first client's subscription migration script and a migration playbook with import templates and a no-write dry run.
Result
29 of 30 tickets delivered, and the first client migrated with it.

Subscription renewals and payment reliability

Context
Some subscription renewals were failing without raising an error, and a race condition could create duplicate payment customers.
What I did
I traced the renewal fault in the worker, designed the fix the team built, and oversaw the repair of affected subscriptions in production. For the race I added distributed locks around customer and payment creation.
Result
Both fixed in production.

LedJun–Oct 2026

Operator work queue for clinic staff

Context
Clinic staff worked orders, patient messages and support tickets in separate places, and the queue slowed down for the largest clinics.
What I did
I specified the queue and its messaging, and bridged support tickets into Jira Service Management. For the queue's performance work I rescoped a planned event-maintained read-model to server-side filtering and pagination in the database. I built the message-template overrides, and diagnosed a fault that hid conversations for a clinic with more than 2,000 open threads; the first fix is live and a follow-up is in review.
Result
Queue and ticket bridge live.

LedAug–Oct 2026

Payment-processor evaluation

Context
The CEO wanted more payment options and was weighing a move to a different processor for better rates.
What I did
I took Apple Pay and Google Pay over the line on the existing processor, including finding why Google Pay wasn't appearing. I then wrote the technical assessment of the two candidates: what each lacked (merchant model, 3-D Secure, card updater) and what a move would cost in integration work. I split the chosen option into seven stories for the team.
Result
Wallets live. The assessment confirmed the CEO's preferred option was workable and set out what it would take; that integration is in progress.

How I work with AI

About 98% of my commits in this period were written with Claude Code. I use it as the implementation layer. Deciding what gets built, checking it and answering for what ships stays with me.

  • I set the approach before any code is written, then review both the diff and the reasoning behind it.
  • Investigations get a second, independent pass before anyone acts on the findings.
  • Every number I report comes out of a script I can rerun, never from the model's summary.
  • AI code review runs on my pull requests, and I resolve its findings before a person reviews the code.
  • I built my own tooling on top of it: commands that turn a specification into tickets, trace a release across seven repositories, and triage support escalations, plus read-only agents that investigate in parallel.

How I run production data changes

  1. Back upCopy every record the change will touch.
  2. Dry runRun the script without writing and count what it would change.
  3. Guarded writeWrite only records that still match the expected state.
  4. VerifyRe-read the data and compare it with the dry run.
  5. Keep a revertLeave a tested script that restores the backup.
